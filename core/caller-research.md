# Identifying a caller (public sources only)

The agent uses this checklist for every number. Results are marked ✅ verified, ❓ likely or ⛔ unknown.

## 1. What the call itself told you
- Name or brand the caller gave (write it down **during** the call)
- Product or service offered
- Callback number or website mentioned
- For texts: sender ID, links (**don't click unknown links**; copy them as text)

This is often the best evidence. Telemarketers usually have to say who they're calling for.

## 2. Number lookups
- Search the full number in quotes, in several formats (e.g. `"0850 XXX XX XX"`, `"0850XXXXXXX"`, `"+90850XXXXXXX"` or `"(202) 555-0147"`, `"2025550147"`, `"+12025550147"`)
- Community complaint sites (region-specific lists are in each region folder)
- Reverse lookup apps' public web pages

⚠️ Community sites are **❓ likely** at best. Different people may report different companies, and spoofed numbers rotate.

## 3. From brand to legal entity
A complaint or demand letter must name the **legal entity**, not just the brand.
- Look at the brand's website footer, privacy policy or terms page. The legal name and address are usually there.
- Check the official business registry for your region (listed in each region folder).
- Lead generators and call centers often call **on behalf of** another company. The seller who benefits can also be responsible. Note both.

## 4. Stop when the trail ends
If none of the above gives a clear answer, mark the entry ⛔ and keep logging. Don't guess. A wrong name in a complaint can make it fail.
